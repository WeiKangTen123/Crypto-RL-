Sharpe Ratio per CPCV Split

Each pair of bars is one CPCV time window. Blue = DRL bot, Red = buy-and-hold. A good model should have blue consistently higher than red across most splits. If all blues are zero, the agent is not placing trades — reward normalisation needs adjustment.

What to look for: consistent outperformance across splits, not just one lucky window. Consistency across time is the whole point of CPCV.
🎯

95% Confidence Interval Comparison

The dot shows the mean Sharpe; the error bar spans the 95% confidence interval. If the two confidence intervals do not overlap, the difference is statistically meaningful. Overlapping intervals suggest the result could be random.

What to look for: DRL CI entirely above HODL CI, and both CIs above zero.
📈

Optuna Optimization History

This chart shows how the objective value improved across 12 Optuna trials. The objective is mean Sharpe(DRL) − mean Sharpe(HODL).

Green dots = new best found. Blue dots = completed trials (not best). A rising best-value line means Optuna is learning which hyperparameters work. A flat line after many trials means the search has converged.

What to look for: an upward trend early on, then convergence. If the line never improves, the hyperparameter search space may need adjustment.
🔍

Hyperparameter Importance

Computed using functional ANOVA — how much variance in the objective is explained by each hyperparameter.

High importance = tuning this parameter moves the needle significantly. Low importance = the model is robust to this parameter's value.

What to look for: focus future tuning effort on the top 2–3 parameters. If learning_rate or batch_size dominate, try a finer search grid for them.
🌐

Hyperparameter Parallel Coordinates

Each line is one trial; colour indicates the objective value (brighter = higher = better). Vertical axes show hyperparameter values.

What to look for: bright lines that share a similar path indicate a promising region — e.g. most high-value trials may cluster at a specific learning rate. This tells you where to focus if you run more trials.

learning_rate — how fast the PPO agent updates its weights. Too high = unstable, too low = slow learning.
batch_size — number of experiences used per gradient update. Larger = more stable but slower.
gamma — discount factor for future rewards. Closer to 1 = agent values long-term gains more.
net_dimension — width of the neural network layers. Larger = more capacity but needs more data.
target_step — steps of environment interaction per training iteration.
break_step — total training steps before stopping each split's training.
norm_reward — scales the reward signal. Too small → agent sees near-zero reward → learns nothing.
